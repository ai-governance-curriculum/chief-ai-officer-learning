# Exercise 05 — Capstone: Year-3 Program Synthesis

**Estimated time**: 5 hours
**Deliverable**: Comprehensive year-3 program view (≤ 6 pages)

---

## The capstone

This is the closing exercise of the
foundational track. You are the CAO of any
of the fictional institutions used
throughout the curriculum (Halverston,
Tessera, Northfield, Aldwych, Sentinel,
Cardinal, Kerridge, Crestbridge, or
Trillium). End of year 3. The Board has
asked for a comprehensive view of where the
program stands.

This exercise is more open-ended than the
others. It is the synthesis of what you
have learned across the track.

## Your assignment

Produce a year-3 program synthesis. The
synthesis is not a board report
([`mod-111`](../../mod-111-board-reporting/README.md)
Exercise 02 is); it is a more comprehensive
document — the Board's CAO-of-the-year
self-summary, written in the posture
Chapter 4 §4.5 describes as the year-3
self-assessment. Sections:

### Section 1 — Where the program is (≤ 1 page)

The current state across the dimensions you
choose to highlight. Drawn from across the
track: maturity
([`mod-111`](../../mod-111-board-reporting/README.md)
Chapter 6); operating effectiveness
([`mod-109`](../../mod-109-compliance-operations/README.md)
+
[`mod-110`](../../mod-110-incident-response/README.md));
risk posture
([`mod-111`](../../mod-111-board-reporting/README.md)
Chapters 2-3); incident history
([`mod-110`](../../mod-110-incident-response/README.md));
compliance state
([`mod-109`](../../mod-109-compliance-operations/README.md));
talent and succession state (Chapter 6);
budget / envelope state (Chapter 7).

Honest assessment per Chapter 4 §4.5 and
[`mod-111`](../../mod-111-board-reporting/README.md)
§6.3 — neither overclaiming nor undue
modesty.

### Section 2 — What changed since year 2 (≤ ¾ page)

The substantive changes in year 3. New
investments; new capabilities; new gaps;
new external context (regulator movement,
market movement, organisational movement).

### Section 3 — What works that is durable (≤ ¾ page)

The structures, practices, and patterns from
the past three years that have proven
durable and should be preserved through
changes ahead. Specific — not "our
governance" but "the AI Risk Council
chairmanship rotation; the 72-hour
notification matrix; the quarterly board
report structure".

### Section 4 — What's fragile (≤ ¾ page)

Honest naming of what is fragile in the
program. Key-person risks; concentrated
vendor dependencies; controls that operate
but haven't been substantively tested;
capacity bottlenecks; capability gaps you
couldn't close (Chapter 6 §6.3).

### Section 5 — What we got wrong (≤ ¾ page)

The choices the CAO and the program have
made over three years that the CAO would,
in hindsight, have made differently. This
is the hardest section to author honestly;
it is also the section that distinguishes
the year-3 synthesis from a year-3
marketing piece. Address errors across at
least two of: operating-model choice
(Chapter 1), year-1 sequencing (Chapter 2
§2.1 and §2.5), year-2 trap handling
(Chapter 3 §3.3), hiring sequence (Chapter
6 §6.2.1), calendar discipline (Chapter 5),
budget framing (Chapter 7 §7.6).

### Section 6 — Year-4 and beyond (≤ ¾ page)

Forward look. Not a year-4 plan; a posture
and direction. What is the program aiming
to do? What capabilities does it need to
develop? What is the CAO's intentional
posture for the next phase? At least one
*strategic* direction (per Chapter 4 §4.5),
not just operational.

### Section 7 — Personal reflection (≤ ¼ page, optional)

If you choose: the CAO's reflection on the
three years. What has the role required of
you personally? What has the role given
you? Where do you go from here? Optional
— if included as a checkbox rather than as
substance, leave it out.

### Section 8 — What the board should know (≤ ¼ page)

Three to five things the Board should know
that aren't otherwise highlighted in the
report.

## Constraints

- The document must be **honest** per the
  [`mod-111`](../../mod-111-board-reporting/README.md)
  §6.3 peer-CAO test and Chapter 4 §4.5.
- "What we got wrong" must contain **at
  least 3 substantive items** addressing at
  least two of the Chapter 1 / 2 / 3 / 5 /
  6 / 7 themes listed in Section 5
  guidance. Programs that surface no errors
  in three years are not credible.
- "What's fragile" must be substantive —
  not trivial gaps. Real fragilities, named.
- "What's durable" must be specific —
  specific structures, not categories.
- The synthesis must not read as a
  marketing piece. It must read as if the
  CAO is writing to a successor who will
  inherit the program (Chapter 6 §6.4.4
  posture).
- Year-4 posture must include at least one
  strategic (not operational) direction
  per Chapter 4 §4.1.5 and §4.5.
- The personal reflection section is
  **optional** — only include if substantive.

## Rubric

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---|
| Where the program is — honest, multi-dimensional | 15% |
| What changed — substantive | 10% |
| What's durable — specific | 15% |
| What's fragile — honest, substantive | 15% |
| What we got wrong — at least 3, across at least 2 themes | 25% |
| Year-4 and beyond — posture + at least one strategic direction | 15% |
| Reads as written-to-successor, not marketing | 5% |

foundational track. You will be a different
reader of these materials in three years

deliberately and defend them when they are
