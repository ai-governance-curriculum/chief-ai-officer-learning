# Exercise 02 — Author a Year-2 Program Review

**Estimated time**: 3 hours
**Deliverable**: Year-2 program review with priorities (≤ 3 pages)

---

## The scenario

You are the CAO at **Tessera Bank**. End of
year 2. The year-1 self-assessment (per
[`mod-111`](../../mod-111-board-reporting/README.md)
Exercise 05 pattern) rated the program at
mid-Developing (CMMI-style maturity level
commonly applied to NIST AI RMF functions
per
[`mod-111`](../../mod-111-board-reporting/README.md)
§6.1.1) across most functions. Year 2 has now completed;
you need to author the year-2 program review
and set year-3 priorities for the Board Risk
Committee.

## Your assignment

Produce a year-2 program review with:

### Section 1 — Year-2 results against the year-2 plan (≤ ¾ page)

Per Chapter 3 §3.1, the six workstreams year
2 should accomplish. For each:

- The year-2 target.
- The actual result.
- The gap (if any).

### Section 2 — Maturity progression (≤ ½ page)

Program maturity per NIST AI RMF function
(GOVERN, MAP, MEASURE, MANAGE), using the
practitioner CMMI-style levels per
[`mod-111`](../../mod-111-board-reporting/README.md)
§6.1.1 — where the program was at start of
year 2 and where it is now. Show at least two
functions progressing at different rates;
uniform progression across all four is almost
always over-rated and should be defended if
claimed.

### Section 3 — Year-2 trap addressed (≤ ½ page)

Per Chapter 3 §3.3, the year-2 trap is
trying to fix every year-1 error
comprehensively. Address whether year 2 fell
into this trap and how it was navigated.
Name at least one specific year-1 error that
was *deferred to year 3* rather than fixed
in year 2 — and defend the deferral.

### Section 4 — First material incident response (≤ ½ page)

Per Chapter 3 §3.4, most programs face their
first material incident in year 2. Address
either:

- The incident that occurred, the response,
  and the post-incident review findings
  (per
  [`mod-110`](../../mod-110-incident-response/README.md)
  Chapter 6); OR
- The near-miss that didn't materialise as
  material — and what the program would have
  done if it had.

### Section 5 — Year-3 priorities (≤ ½ page)

Per Chapter 4 §4.1, the five workstreams
year 3 should accomplish — calibrated to
Tessera's year-2 maturity state. Ordered by
priority with the reasoning for the order.

### Section 6 — Stalling-watch (≤ ¼ page)

Per Chapter 4 §4.3, the warning signs of
year-3 stalling. For each of the five
symptoms (§4.3.1 through §4.3.5), an honest
assessment of whether Tessera is showing it.
At least one honest "yes" or "early signs"
answer is expected; see constraints.

## Constraints

- Year-2 results must be **honest** — per
  [`mod-111`](../../mod-111-board-reporting/README.md)
  Chapter 6. Programs that show only
  successes are not credible.
- The maturity progression must be
  **specific** — not "improved" but "Level 2
  (Developing) to Level 3 (Defined) on
  GOVERN".
- The first-material-incident treatment must
  be substantive — name the incident, the
  response, the findings. "No incidents
  occurred" is itself a Chapter 4 §4.3.5
  finding and must be addressed as such.
- Year-3 priorities must be **prioritised** —
  at most 5, with reasoning for the priority
  order.
- The stalling-watch must include at least
  one honest finding that a symptom is
  present or in early stages — programs that
  show no warning signs are almost certainly
  not seeing themselves clearly (Chapter 4
  §4.3.6).

## Rubric

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---|
| Year-2 results — honest treatment of all six | 25% |
| Maturity progression — specific, uneven | 15% |
| Year-2 trap addressed with named deferral | 10% |
| First material incident treatment | 15% |
| Year-3 priorities — prioritised | 20% |
| Stalling-watch — at least one honest finding | 15% |
