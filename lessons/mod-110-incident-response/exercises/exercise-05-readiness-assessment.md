# Exercise 05 — Organizational Incident-Readiness Assessment

**Estimated time**: 3 hours
**Deliverable**: Readiness assessment (≤ 4 pages)

---

## The scenario

You are advising **Aldwych Health** (continuation
from mod-101 / mod-102 / mod-103 / mod-104 — the
UK healthcare system with EU AI Act exposure
via its cross-border teaching-hospital
affiliation). Aldwych has implemented the
governance, monitoring, classification,
notification, and review machinery from earlier
modules. The Audit Committee has asked for an
**honest readiness assessment** before the system
is considered operational and before the Audit
Committee acknowledges the AI program as
externally defensible.

## Your assignment

Produce a readiness assessment in four sections.

### Section 1 — Scope and method (≤ ¼ page)

- What is being assessed — Aldwych's AI
  incident-response readiness across people,
  process, and technology.
- The method — interview, document review,
  evidence sampling, tabletop observation,
  prior-incident analysis (if any).
- The readiness scale used (define it
  explicitly; e.g., Not Ready / Partial /
  Mostly Ready / Fully Ready with specific
  anchor criteria for each level).

### Section 2 — The dimensions assessed (≤ 2½ pages)

Assess at least **nine** dimensions of readiness.
For each dimension, provide: current state with
specific evidence, maturity rating, top gap, and
recommendations.

1. **Detection capability** — can incidents be
   detected reliably and quickly across the
   Chapter 2 §2.1 channels.
2. **Classification capability** — can the
   first-hour team classify per
   [`mod-107`](../../mod-107-ai-security/README.md)
   §6 without negotiation.
3. **Containment capability** — can the team
   execute the Chapter 3 §3.1 options at the
   right proportionate level, with the
   Chapter 3 §3.5 decision record.
4. **Investigation capability** — can the team
   investigate with the Chapter 5 §5.1
   properties and the NTSB discipline
   (Chapter 5 §5.4).
5. **Notification capability** — can the
   notification matrix (Chapter 4) be operated
   under pressure, including multi-
   jurisdiction coordination.
6. **Communication capability** — the four
   audiences of Chapter 8 (CEO, Board, press /
   public, customers) and internal employees.
7. **Documentation capability** — evidence
   produced contemporaneously (per
   [`mod-108`](../../mod-108-audit-ledgers-and-evidence/README.md))
   and retrievable.
8. **Post-incident review capability** — can
   the review discipline (Chapter 6) produce
   artifacts that survive external scrutiny
   (Chapter 6 §6.7).
9. **Tabletop readiness** — the Chapter 7
   discipline operating: cadence, scenario
   quality, documentation, routed
   recommendations.

For each dimension:

- **Current state** — specific evidence
  supporting the assessment, not just an
  assertion. Reference specific artifacts,
  specific exercises, specific prior
  incidents or near-misses, or specific gaps
  observed.
- **Maturity rating** per the scale defined
  in Section 1.
- **Top gap(s)** at this dimension.
- **Recommendations for improvement** —
  specific, assigned, and scheduled.

### Section 3 — Aldwych-specific concerns (≤ ¾ page)

Four specific concerns for Aldwych the generic
dimensions do not fully capture:

- **Clinical-authority boundary.** The CMO
  and the AI program's response intersect
  materially in clinical incidents. Who leads
  which decisions? How is the single-named-
  lead convention held when clinical
  authority is operating in parallel?
- **Patient-affected notifications.** Patient
  communication has clinical-communication
  implications (duty of candour under the
  UK CQC regulations, informed-consent
  implications, individual-care continuity).
  How does Chapter 8's customer
  communication adapt to the clinical
  context?
- **Multi-regulator landscape.** MHRA
  (medical-device regulation), CQC (care
  quality), ICO (information commissioner,
  UK GDPR), and — via the teaching-hospital
  affiliation — potentially EU AI Act
  authorities, all potentially engaged at
  once. The notification matrix is more
  complex than the generic Chapter 4
  template.
- **Public-service context.** Aldwych
  operates in a system where public trust
  and political attention operate
  differently from private-sector firms.
  Enterprise-face communication (Chapter 8)
  has additional dimensions.

Address each substantively — not just listed
but with specific implications for the
readiness assessment.

### Section 4 — Overall readiness verdict (≤ ½ page)

A clear overall verdict with three elements:

- **Rating.** Not Ready / Partial / Mostly
  Ready / Fully Ready, with reasoning.
- **Named blockers or gaps.** Specific items
  that must be resolved before moving up a
  level. For a Mostly-Ready verdict, the
  blockers to Fully-Ready; for Partial, the
  blockers to Mostly-Ready.
- **Specific actions required.** Each action
  with an owner, a timeline, and the
  signal that would confirm the action
  is complete.

## Constraints

- The assessment must be **honest** —
  acknowledging gaps where they exist. A
  fully-positive readiness assessment for a
  newly-implemented program is implausible
  and will not survive the next real
  incident.
- Each dimension must include **specific
  evidence** supporting the rating, not just
  assertions. "Monitoring is in place" is
  not evidence; "the demographic-parity
  monitor on the triage system has alerted
  twice in the last quarter, both
  classified as false positives in the audit
  ledger" is.
- The Aldwych-specific concerns must be
  addressed substantively, not just listed.
- The overall verdict must be specific.
  "Mostly ready with caveats" is not
  specific; "Mostly ready; the three named
  blockers must be resolved before the Audit
  Committee acknowledges the program as
  operational" is.
- The assessment must identify at least one
  blocker that is **procedural / policy**
  rather than technical. Programs whose
  gaps are all technical have usually under-
  surveyed the policy and process layer.

## Rubric

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---|
| Method specified with explicit readiness scale | 10% |
| Nine dimensions covered with evidence-based ratings | 30% |
| Aldwych-specific concerns addressed substantively | 20% |
| Overall verdict specific and defended with named blockers | 20% |
| Honest acknowledgment of gaps, including at least one procedural / policy blocker | 15% |
| Length discipline — ≤ 4 pages | 5% |
