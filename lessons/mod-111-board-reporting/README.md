# Module 111 — Board Reporting and Risk Appetite

> Module 111 of the Chief AI Officer track.
> Where everything downstream rolls up to
> executive accountability — the risk appetite
> statement, the quarterly board report, the
> materiality framework, the CAO at the board
> table, the annual self-assessment, and the
> portfolio P&L/ROI view that completes the
> board's picture of the AI program.

## What you will leave with

After working through this module you should be
able to:

1. Distinguish what boards need from CAO
   reporting from what they don't — and avoid
   the common over-reporting failure mode.
2. Draft an AI risk appetite statement that
   survives board review and operates against
   actual decisions.
3. Author a quarterly AI report that the Board
   Risk Committee can act on — short,
   structured, honest.
4. Apply a materiality framework that
   determines when an AI matter reaches the
   board versus stays at the operational level.
5. Operate the CAO at the board table — what
   to say, what to ask for, how to handle
   pressure.
6. Conduct an annual program self-assessment
   that surfaces meaningful findings rather
   than confirming the program is operating.
7. Consolidate AI portfolio P&L attribution
   and ROI reporting for board / audit-
   committee / CFO consumption.

## A note on tone

Board reporting is the module where the CAO
function most visibly either earns the board's
confidence or loses it over time. The chapters
hold to that stakes level. The specific
disciplines — the three-page test, the risk
appetite patterns, the materiality framework,
the honesty discipline at the table, the
honesty test for the self-assessment, the
portfolio reconciliation to the CFO's books
— are not procedural niceties. They are the
machinery that decides whether the AI program
is seen as substantively run or as a
compliance filing with a function attached.
