# Exercise 04 — Prepare for an Unexpected Board Ask

**Estimated time**: 2 hours
**Deliverable**: Internal briefing + prepared response (≤ 2 pages combined)

---

## The scenario

You are the CAO at **Sentinel Mutual Bank**,
a $55B US regional bank with primary OCC
supervision, CFPB consumer-protection
oversight, and a growing book of AI-driven
decisioning in retail credit, deposit account
opening, and fraud screening.

The Board Risk Committee chair calls you on
Tuesday afternoon: at Thursday's committee
meeting, the board plans to ask you a
specific question that wasn't on the agenda.
The chair has shared the question in advance
to give you time to prepare:

> *"In light of recent industry incidents
> involving AI-driven adverse actions and
> regulatory responses, can the CAO explain
> Sentinel's specific exposure to a similar
> event, and what specifically the program
> is doing to mitigate that exposure?"*

You have 36 hours.

## Your assignment

Produce two artifacts.

### Artifact 1 — Internal briefing (≤ 1 page)

A briefing for your own preparation — not
delivered to the board. Four sections:

- **The question behind the question.** What
  is the board really asking? (Chapter 5
  §5.5 — handling pressure in the room; the
  surface question often has a different
  underlying concern.)
- **The honest assessment of Sentinel's
  exposure.** What is real, what is
  manageable, what is concerning. Chapter 1
  §1.2 (what boards need) and Chapter 5 §5.3
  (honesty discipline) apply.
- **What you should not say.** True things
  that would not advance the conversation if
  volunteered — per the
  [`mod-102`](../../mod-102-regulatory-landscape/README.md)
  Exercise 04 "things-not-to-say"
  discipline.
- **The 24-hour follow-up plan.** What you
  commit to delivering after the meeting.

### Artifact 2 — Prepared response (≤ 1 page)

Your prepared response to deliver at the
meeting. Three to five paragraphs,
deliverable in five minutes:

1. **Direct opening.** Name the specific
   exposure in a sentence or two.
2. **Specific risks identified.** The
   substantive risks, named.
3. **Specific mitigations in place or
   being built.** Not generic — specific
   control and process references.
4. **Specific gaps or uncertainties.** The
   §5.3.1 overclaiming anti-pattern applies
   — gaps must be named.
5. **The closing ask of the board** (if any).

## Constraints

- The response must be **specific** — no
  generic language about AI risk. Reg B
  §1002.9 specificity, ECOA, UDAAP, and the
  CFPB's AI-adjacent enforcement posture
  should be named by name where relevant.
- The response must include **at least one
  honest concession** — something Sentinel
  is not yet doing or has not yet resolved.
- The response must explicitly avoid the
  Chapter 5 §5.3 anti-patterns —
  overclaiming, hedging, education-as-
  answer.
- The "what you should not say" section
  must be **substantive** — not just trivial
  facts. It should surface real tactical
  choices (internal findings not yet
  disclosed to regulator, pending model
  decisions, specific peer-institution
  intelligence that is not yet actionable).
- The response must be **deliverable in 5
  minutes** at the meeting; not a
  20-minute educational presentation.
- The 24-hour follow-up plan must be
  **specific** — specific artifact,
  specific delivery to specific
  committee role, specific date.

## Rubric

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---|
| Question-behind-the-question identified | 20% |
| Honest exposure assessment | 25% |
| Specific risks named | 15% |
| Specific mitigations named | 15% |
| Honest concession included | 10% |
| Response deliverable in 5 minutes | 5% |
| Chapter 5 §5.3 anti-patterns avoided | 5% |
| 24-hour follow-up plan is specific | 5% |
